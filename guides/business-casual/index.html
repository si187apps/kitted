Business casual for men, explained

Office-smart without the full suit.

By Kitted · 6 November 2026 · 2 min read

Business casual is the default in many UK offices, and for work events outside the boardroom. You're showing you're at work, without the formality of a suit and tie.

What to wear, piece by piece

Jacket
A blazer is optional but useful for meetings. Navy, grey or a subtle check.
Top
A shirt or smart polo, no tie needed. A fine knit over a shirt works in colder months.
Trousers
Chinos or wool trousers in grey, navy, stone or olive.
Shoes
Leather shoes, loafers or suede boots.
Extras
A belt that matches your shoes, a tidy bag, and a jacket on the back of your chair for unexpected meetings.

What to avoid

  • Jeans and trainers, unless the office is clearly relaxed.
  • T-shirts with logos or slogans.
  • Shorts and sandals, even in summer.

Two ways to wear it

Keep it classic

Navy blazer, white Oxford shirt, grey wool trousers and brown leather Derbies.

Push it a little

Grey merino crew neck over a light blue shirt, olive chinos and brown suede Chelsea boots.

Quick questions

Is a polo shirt okay?

A smart knitted or piqué polo in a plain colour is fine. Tuck it in if you're wearing a jacket.

What about Fridays?

Most offices relax further. Dark jeans with a shirt and blazer are usually safe.

Do I need a blazer every day?

No. Keep one at work for meetings and presentations, and wear a shirt or knit the rest of the time.
