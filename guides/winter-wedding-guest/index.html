What to wear as a winter wedding guest

Smart and celebratory, and warm enough to enjoy the photos outside.

By Togged · 24 November 2026 · 3 min read

A winter wedding, anywhere from November to February, follows the same rules as any other: read the invitation, dress a notch smarter than you think you need, and leave the spotlight to the couple. What changes is the cloth, the colours and the coat. Expect a cold church, photos outside in the wind and possibly rain, then a warm venue for the evening. If the invite names a dress code, that wins; our lounge suit and black tie guides cover those.

What to wear, piece by piece

Suit
A suit in a heavier cloth: worsted wool, flannel or a subtle tweed. Charcoal, navy, deep brown or a muted check all suit winter light. A good off-the-peg wool suit is around £150–£300; for an evening reception, a velvet jacket in navy, bottle green or burgundy over dark trousers is a strong choice.
Shirt and tie
White or pale blue cotton shirts still work best. Ties can go richer for winter: wool, knitted or a deep silk in burgundy, forest green or navy. Wear one for the ceremony; you can lose it for the evening.
Coat
A wool overcoat that fits over your suit jacket and covers it, in navy, charcoal or camel. It will be in the photos outside the church, so it matters. Expect around £80–£200 for a decent wool-blend coat. A waxed or quilted jacket looks wrong over a suit.
Shoes
Leather Derbies, Oxfords or smart Chelsea boots with a rubber or grippy sole for wet grass and gravel. Dark brown or black. Polish and waterproof them the night before; suede is a gamble in the rain.
Extras
A wool or cashmere-blend scarf in a plain colour, leather gloves, dark wool socks and a pocket square. A thin merino layer under the shirt helps on long days outside without adding bulk.

What to avoid

  • All white or cream: that belongs to the couple, winter or not.
  • A puffer or outdoor jacket over your suit. It ruins the photos.
  • Thin summer suits and linen. You will be cold, and they look out of season.
  • Brand-new leather soles on wet stone steps.

Two ways to wear it

Keep it classic

Charcoal wool suit, white shirt, a burgundy silk tie, black Oxfords with a grippy sole and a navy wool overcoat with a grey scarf. Right for almost any church or hotel wedding.

Push it a little

Brown flannel or subtle tweed suit, pale blue shirt, a forest green knitted tie, dark brown leather Chelsea boots and a camel overcoat. Swap the tie for an open collar in the evening.

Quick questions

Can I wear velvet?

Yes, for an evening ceremony or reception. A velvet jacket in a deep colour over dark wool trousers is festive and smart. For a daytime church ceremony, keep velvet to the evening.

Is a three-piece suit too much?

Not in winter. A waistcoat adds warmth and looks smart when the jacket comes off later. Keep it in the same cloth as the suit.

How can I do this on a budget?

Wear a suit you already own in navy or charcoal and spend on a richer wool tie, around £20–£40, and a smart scarf. If you need a coat, a plain navy wool-blend overcoat at around £80–£120 will last for years of weddings and work.
